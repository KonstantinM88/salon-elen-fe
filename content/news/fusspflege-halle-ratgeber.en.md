## Cosmetic, intensive or medical foot care: what is the difference?

**Cosmetic foot care and pedicure** focus on comfort, grooming and the appearance of otherwise uncomplicated feet. Depending on the booked service, this may include shortening and shaping the nails, caring for cuticles and nail folds, removing normal callus and applying finishing care.

**Intensive foot care** reserves more time when the skin and heels need extra attention. It can be the better choice when a short standard appointment is unlikely to be enough.

**Medical foot care within the services offered** is intended for more demanding foot and nail concerns. Examples include thickened nails, corns, heel fissures, an ingrown nail or targeted pressure relief. The exact scope depends on the visible condition. It does not replace diagnosis or medical treatment by a doctor.

## Common worries before an appointment

### “I am embarrassed about my feet.”

Callus, dry skin, uneven nails and hard-working heels are common reasons to book an appointment. You do not need to make your feet “salon ready” first. Honest information about sensitive areas and what bothers you is much more useful.

### “I am worried that treatment may hurt.”

Tell your specialist immediately if an area is tender or anything feels uncomfortable. Good care considers your comfort as well as the visible result.

### “I do not know which service to book.”

Start with your most important goal: nails only, a complete pedicure, heavier callus, one specific nail concern or an at-home visit. If necessary, the scope can be discussed before treatment and a suitable follow-up appointment recommended.

### “I have little time or limited mobility.”

An express pedicure is available for a compact nail appointment. If travelling is physically or practically difficult, [mobile foot care within Halle](/fusspflege-halle?lang=en#mobile-fusspflege) is available; travel within the city is included in the displayed price.

## How should I prepare for professional foot care?

- Arrive with clean, dry feet and, where possible, without freshly applied cream.
- Do not aggressively remove existing polish if the nails or surrounding skin are sensitive; mention the finish when booking.
- Do not cut deeply into the corners of a problematic nail shortly before the appointment.
- Before treatment, disclose pain, allergies, diabetes, circulation or sensitivity problems and any changes that require medical assessment.
- If you have booked nail polish or gel polish, bring open or sufficiently wide footwear when appropriate for your chosen finish.

## Aftercare: helping the comfortable feeling last

Simple regular habits are usually more helpful than occasional aggressive treatment:

- Dry the feet carefully after washing, especially between the toes.
- Moisturise dry skin appropriately without leaving slippery amounts of product between the toes.
- Do not cut into corns, deep cracks or sensitive nail edges yourself.
- Check shoes for pressure points, rubbing seams and enough room for the toes.
- Monitor changes and do not wait until the next cosmetic appointment if pain, inflammation or a wound develops.

For people with diabetes, even a small pressure point or injury can be significant. German public-health information recommends regular foot checks and early medical assessment of changes. Further information is available from [gesund.bund.de](https://gesund.bund.de/diabetischer-fuss) and [DiabInfo](https://www.diabinfo.de/fileadmin/diabinfo/Grafiken/diabinfo_Fusspflege_EN.pdf).

> **Important safety note:** Seek medical advice before cosmetic treatment if you have an open wound, acute inflammation, bleeding, severe pain, a sudden unexplained skin or nail change, or a medically relevant high-risk foot. Salon Elen does not provide medical diagnoses.

### How long does a pedicure take?

### What is the difference between a classic and an apparatus pedicure?

A classic pedicure combines care for nails, nail folds, cuticles and normal callus. An apparatus pedicure uses suitable equipment for precise treatment. The better option depends on your preferred process and care needs.

### Can heavier callus be removed?

Yes. Normal and intensive callus care and intensive foot-care appointments are available. Deep cracks, inflammation, bleeding or severe pain should be medically assessed first.

### Can I book with an ingrown toenail?

The catalogue includes a targeted service for an ingrown nail. Whether it is suitable depends on the condition. Please seek medical care first if there is acute inflammation, pus, bleeding or severe pain.

### Is care available for a nail that looks unusual?

Supportive cosmetic care for a visibly changed nail may be possible. The cause must be medically assessed; cosmetic foot care cannot replace diagnosis or necessary treatment.

### Is foot care possible if I have diabetes?

There is no universal answer. Diabetes, circulation problems or reduced sensation must be disclosed before treatment. Existing damage, wounds or unexplained changes should first be assessed medically so that the appropriate care can be determined.

### Can I book a pedicure with nail polish or gel polish?

Yes. Separate appointments with classic nail polish or gel polish are available, allowing the required additional time to be planned from the start.

### How often should I have professional foot care?

There is no single interval for everyone. Nail growth, callus formation, footwear, daily strain, the season and your personal goals all matter. A realistic rhythm is easier to recommend after the first appointment.

### Can I book a home visit?

Yes. Mobile foot care is available within Halle. Choose a mobile service directly in the catalogue so that treatment time and travel are planned correctly.
